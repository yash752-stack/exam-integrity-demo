Exam-Hall Integrity AI

Yash Chaudhary, 2026 · GitHub · LinkedIn

An unsupervised detector for malpractice in exam-hall CCTV. Labelled footage of cheating does not exist at any useful scale, so the system learns what ordinary movement looks like in each room and raises the movement that is rare. A reviewer then checks a short list of clips instead of hours of video.

Method

Each stage is a standard component chosen for this footage: steep camera angles, forty people per room, and back-row candidates only a few pixels tall.

CCTV frames, 3 fps
  → YOLO11x-pose          17-keypoint skeleton for every person
  → ByteTrack             one identity per candidate, tuned for small, faint people
  → rarity scoring        Fisher score of each person's 45 s movement windows
                          against the rest of the room, plus a stand-up detector
  → filters               invigilators, occlusion, track glitches; thresholds frozen
  → Qwen3-VL-8B (4-bit)   verifies that each seat detection is a real person
  → reviewer              confirms or dismisses each short clip

Decisions

Rarity scoring instead of a supervised classifier.
There is no labelled malpractice footage at scale, and ordinary movement differs by room, camera angle and seating, so each room is scored against itself.
No second pose model on seat crops.
Running RTMPose-x on every seat raised the people seen per frame from 13 to 24 and found no additional malpractice. Seat crops freeze exactly the movements worth flagging: a person standing up stays squashed inside their seat box. The simpler pipeline stayed.
The vision-language model verifies; it does not decide.
Qwen3-VL confirms that a seat holds a real person. Whether someone is a seated candidate is decided by their trajectory, so a single model answer cannot override the geometry.
A person makes the final call.
At roughly one confirmed alert in three, review takes seconds per clip. Keeping a human in the loop lets the detector favour recall over precision.

Limits

Most misses are small head turns and leaning in the back rows, where a person is only a few pixels tall. The code and footage are private because the recordings belong to the exam body; I am glad to walk through the implementation.
